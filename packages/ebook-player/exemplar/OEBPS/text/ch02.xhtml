2. Inside the leaf

A leaf is a food factory, and like any factory it is laid out for the job.

By the end of this page you should be able to

  1. Write the word equation for photosynthesis.
  2. Describe how a leaf's structure suits it for photosynthesis.

Look at the layer of tall cells just under the upper surface. These are the palisade cells, and they are packed with chloroplasts: tiny green structures containing chlorophyll, the pigment that absorbs light. Being near the top puts them where the light is strongest.

Underneath, the holes in the lower surface are stomata. They open to let carbon dioxide in and oxygen out, and close to stop the leaf drying out. The vein in the middle is the plumbing: one set of tubes brings water up from the roots, another carries the sugar away to wherever the plant needs it.

The word equation

Scientists sum the whole process up in one line. Read it as "carbon dioxide and water, using light energy, become glucose and oxygen":

carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen
(light energy, chlorophyll)

Try it: the starch test

A leaf that has been photosynthesising stores some of its glucose as starch, and starch turns iodine solution blue-black. Cover part of a leaf with foil for two days, then pick it, soften it in hot water, remove the green colour with warm ethanol, rinse, and add iodine. Which part turns blue-black, and why?
