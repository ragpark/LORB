1. What is photosynthesis?

Every meal you have ever eaten started with a plant turning light into food.

By the end of this page you should be able to

  1. Say what photosynthesis is and why it matters for every living thing.
  2. Name the two things a plant takes in and the two things it makes.
  3. Explain why photosynthesis only happens in the light.

Animals eat to get energy. Plants cannot eat, so they make their own food. The process they use is called photosynthesis. The word comes from Greek: photo means light and synthesis means putting together. A plant puts together food using light.

To do it, a plant needs two raw materials and one source of energy:

Carbon dioxide
A gas in the air. The plant takes it in through tiny holes in its leaves.
Water
Absorbed by the roots and carried up the stem to the leaves.
Light
Usually sunlight. The energy in the light is what drives the reaction, which is why a plant kept in a dark cupboard soon turns yellow and dies.

From these, the plant makes two things: glucose, a sugar it uses for energy and for building new leaves, stems and roots, and oxygen, which it releases into the air. That oxygen is the oxygen you are breathing now.

Did you know?

Nearly all the oxygen in the atmosphere was put there by photosynthesis. About half of it comes not from trees but from microscopic algae floating in the oceans.
